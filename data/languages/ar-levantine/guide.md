# Levantine Arabic (شامي)

The shared core of Syrian, Lebanese, Jordanian and Palestinian Arabic. Country guides override these notes wherever they differ.

## Pronunciation

- ق varies: a glottal stop (ء) in most cities, /g/ in rural and Bedouin speech (strong in Jordan), /q/ in some communities. In chat it is almost always still written ق ("قلتلك").
- In urban speech ث often becomes ت or س, and ذ becomes د or ز ("تلاتة"، "هادا"). Chat spelling mixes phonetic (تلاتة) and standard (ثلاثة) forms; both are normal.
- Short final vowels drop and long ones shorten, so written forms are often clipped ("شو بدك"، "كيفك").

## Grammar & markers

- Negation of verbs: ما ("ما بعرف"، "ما في"). Negation of nouns and adjectives differs by country (مش or مو); see the country guide.
- Future: رح ("رح إجي بكرا"). Some areas also use راح or حـ.
- Present/habitual prefix: بـ ("بحكي"، "بتعرف؟"، "بنروح").
- Want: بدّ + suffix ("بدّي"، "بدّك"، "بدّنا").
- There is / there isn't: في / ما في.
- Shared everyday words: كتير (a lot), منيح (good), هيك (like this), بكرا (tomorrow), مبارح (yesterday), لسّا (still/not yet), خلص (enough, done), يلّا.
- Question words: شو (what), وين (where), ليش (why), إيمتى (when), مين (who), كيف (how), قدّيش (how much). Some vary by country.
- Demonstratives ("this", "these") differ by country; see the country guide.

## Common AI mistakes

- Egyptian words creeping in: دلوقتي (use the local "now"), إزيّك (use كيفك), أوي (use كتير), عايز (use بدّي), إيه (use شو).
- Gulf words creeping in: وايد (use كتير), أبغى (use بدّي), الحين.
- MSA mid-sentence: سوف (use رح), لماذا (use ليش), ماذا (use شو), ليس (use مش or مو), أريد (use بدّي).
- Treating "Levantine" as one dialect and blending Syrian, Lebanese, Jordanian and Palestinian forms in one reply (for example هيدا next to هاد, or هلّق next to هسّا). Pick the user's country and stay in it.

## Natural usage

- Mirror the user's register, length and script. Many users write in Arabizi (Latin letters with 2 = ء, 3 = ع, 7 = ح, 5 = خ); answer in the same script they use.
- Everyday chat is dialect, not MSA. Only switch to MSA if the user does, or for formal writing they ask for.
- Do not stack slang or emotive phrases in every sentence; a normal speaker uses them occasionally.
