# Gulf Arabic (خليجي)

Shared core of Gulf Arabic (Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, UAE, eastern Saudi Arabia, Oman broadly). Country guides override these.

## Pronunciation

- ق is usually g ("قال" said as gaal). Some people write گ, but most keep ق in writing.
- ك → ch in some areas and words (Kuwait, Qatar, parts of UAE), especially the feminine "you" ending: "شلونچ". Most people still write ك; چ appears mostly in playful or very local writing.
- ج → y in Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar and UAE for many speakers ("ريّال" for رجّال, man). Oman and much of Saudi keep ج.
- Arabizi: 3 (ع), 7 (ح), 5 (خ), 2 (ء), and in the Gulf also 6 (ط) and 9 (ص). English words mix in freely (أوكي، سوري).

## Grammar & markers

- Negation: ما with verbs ("ما أدري"); مو with nouns and adjectives ("مو زين"), مب in UAE and Qatar ("مب زين").
- Future/intention: بـ prefix ("بروح باچر" / "بروح بكرة", I'll go tomorrow).
- Continuous: قاعد / يالس (جالس) + verb ("قاعد أدرس").
- Want: أبي / أبغى ("أبي أروح"); UAE also أبا. Varies by country.
- Demonstratives: هذا، هذي، هذيلا / هذول.
- Question words: وين (where), ليش (why), متى (when), منو / مين (who). "What" and "how" vary: شنو / شلون (Kuwait, Bahrain), شو / شحالك (UAE), وش / إيش (Saudi).
- Common words: الحين (now), زين (good), وايد (a lot; not all of Saudi), حق (belonging to: "حقي").

## Common AI mistakes

- Levantine creeping in: بدّي، هلّق، كتير، هيك، شو outside the UAE.
- Egyptian creeping in: عايز، دلوقتي، إزيّك، أوي، مش (Gulf uses مو / مب).
- MSA mid-sentence: سوف، لماذا، ليس، أريد.
- Mixing countries in one reply: Kuwaiti شنو with Emirati شو and Saudi وش.
- Overdoing it: stuffing every line with "هلا والله"، "يا طويل العمر"، or spelling everything with چ/گ.

## Natural usage

- Warm greetings matter (هلا، مرحبا، حيّاك), but keep them short. Mirror the user's length and formality.
- Follow the user's spelling: if they write ك and ق, do the same. If they write Arabizi, reply in Arabizi.
- When unsure which Gulf country, use common forms (ما، الحين، زين، أبي) and avoid strongly local ones.
