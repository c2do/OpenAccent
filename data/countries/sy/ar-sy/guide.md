# Syrian Arabic (سوري)

Syrian Arabic, with Damascene (شامي) as the default. Aleppo, the coast and rural areas differ; follow the user if they show a regional form.

## Pronunciation

- ق is a glottal stop in Damascus ("قال" said as "آل"). Usually still written ق; some write أ ("أديش").
- ج is a soft "zh" sound, not /g/ or /dj/. Written ج.
- Final ة often sounds like -e after most consonants ("مدرسة" = madrase), but it is written ة.

## Grammar & markers

- Negation of nouns and adjectives: مو ("مو هيك"، "مو مشكلة"، "مو عارف"). Verbs: ما ("ما بعرف").
- Future: رح ("رح روح").
- Continuous: عم ("عم ياكل"، "عم إحكي").
- Demonstratives: هاد (this, m.), هي or هاي (this, f.), هدول (these); هداك / هديك (that).
- Pronouns: نحنا (we), إنتو (you pl.), هنّي / هنن (they).
- Now: هلّق / هلأ. Also common: شلون or كيف (how), قدّيش / أدّيش (how much), إي (yes), لك (attention-getter: "لك شو عم تحكي؟").

## Common AI mistakes

- Using Lebanese هيدا / هيدي instead of هاد / هي, or Lebanese French-flavoured chat ("bonjour"، "ça va") as if it were Syrian.
- Using مش where Damascenes say مو. مش is heard in Syria but مو is the Damascene default.
- Using Jordanian or Palestinian هسّا / إشي instead of هلّق / شي.
- Egyptian, Gulf or MSA leakage as listed in the Levantine guide.

## Natural usage

- Damascene speech is warm and polite; phrases like "تكرم"، "تقبرني"، "يسلمو" are common but should not be piled on.
- Mirror the user's spelling: many write هلق or هلأ, هيك, شو without hamza or shadda.
