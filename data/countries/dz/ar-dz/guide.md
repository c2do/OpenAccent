# Algerian Arabic (الدارجة الجزائرية)

Algerian Darja, with Algiers speech as the default. Oran (west) and Constantine (east) differ; note their forms only if the user uses them.

## Pronunciation

- Algiers: ق is q; many other regions say g. ث and ذ usually become ت and د ("تلاتة"، "هادا").
- Arabizi is very common: 3, 7, 9 (ق), kh, ch, ou, and dj for ج ("djib"). Common chat spellings: "wech", "rani", "drk", "bezzaf", "sahit".
- French is mixed in constantly, often whole phrases ("normal", "déjà", "c'est bon"); French-origin words written in Arabic too (بورطابل، طوموبيل، ميرسي).

## Grammar & markers

- Negation: ما...ش ("ما نعرفش"); with nouns ماشي ("ماشي هكذا").
- Continuous and "I am": راني، راك، راه، راهي + verb or adjective ("راني نخدم"، "راني مليح").
- Future: often just the plain verb from context, or رايح + verb ("رايح نروح"). Oran and the west also use غادي.
- Want: حبيت / نحب, and بغيت ("حبيت نروح"). Usage varies by region.
- Demonstratives: هادا، هادي، هادو; that: هداك، هديك، هدوك.
- Question words: واش (what; "واش راك؟" = how are you), وين (where), علاش، كيفاش، شكون، وقتاش (when), شحال (how much).
- Common words: درك / ضرك (now), بزاف (a lot), مليح (good), صح (right), تاع (of), "يعطيك الصحة" / "صحّيت" (thanks).

## Common AI mistakes

- Moroccan forms: كا / تا (continuous), غادي in Algiers speech, دابا، ديال، مزيان، فين، واخا.
- Tunisian forms: توّا، برشا، باهي، متاع، باش (future), قاعد (continuous), شنوّة، موش.
- Egyptian, Levantine and Gulf words (عايز، بدّي، شو، أبي) and MSA "لا أعرف"، "سوف".
- Mixing Algiers with Oranais or Constantinois features in one reply.
- Over-formal replies to a casual French-mixed message, or overdoing street slang.

## Natural usage

- Mirror the user's mix of Arabic script, Arabizi and French; many Algerians write mostly in Latin letters.
- Keep it relaxed and direct; "خويا" / "ختي" (brother/sister) are friendly but not needed in every line.
