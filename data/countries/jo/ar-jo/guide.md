# Jordanian Arabic (أردني)

Jordanian Arabic: urban Ammani as the default, plus common rural and Bedouin-influenced features. Jordanian and Palestinian speech overlap heavily.

## Pronunciation

- ق varies a lot: /g/ is very common (especially among men and in rural or Bedouin speech), a glottal stop is common in urban Amman (especially among women). In chat it is almost always written ق.
- ج is /dʒ/ ("j"), not the Syrian/Lebanese "zh".
- Bedouin-influenced speech keeps ث and ذ, and often says ظ for ض. Some rural speech turns ك into "ch" (e.g. كيف as "cheef"); do not imitate this unless the user writes it.

## Grammar & markers

- Negation of nouns and adjectives: مش ("مش عارف"، "مش هيك"). Verbs: ما ("ما بعرف").
- Future: رح or راح ("راح أحكيلك").
- Present: بـ ("بحكي"). عم is heard in Amman but much less than in Syria/Lebanon.
- Demonstratives: هاد / هادا (this, m.), هاي (this, f.), هدول (these). Rural/Bedouin: هاظ / هاض, هظول.
- Pronouns: إحنا (we), إنتو (you pl.), هم (they).
- Now: هسّا / هسّع. Thing: إشي ("في إشي؟"، "ولا إشي"). Because: عشان. What: شو, also إيش.
- Common: زلمة (man, "يا زلمة"), يعطيك العافية, ماشي, طيب, والله.

## Common AI mistakes

- Using Syrian/Lebanese هلّق, مو, هيدا or عم in every sentence instead of هسّا, مش, هاد and plain بـ.
- Writing "Bedouin" features (هاظ, ك as چ) for an urban user, or urban Ammani forms for a user who writes rural ones. Follow the user's cues.
- Egyptian, Gulf or MSA leakage as listed in the Levantine guide.

## Natural usage

- Tone is direct and friendly; "يا زلمة" and "والله" are frequent, but do not overuse them.
- Mirror spelling: many write هسا or هسه, اشي, عشان without hamza or shadda.
