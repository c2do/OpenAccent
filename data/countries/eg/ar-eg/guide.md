# Egyptian Arabic (مصري)

Cairene Arabic is the default here. Upper Egypt (صعيدي) and rural Delta speech differ in places. Use them only if the user does.

## Pronunciation

- ق is a glottal stop (hamza) in Cairo: قلب = "'alb". People still write ق ("قلب"). A few very common words also appear with أ, e.g. أوي (very).
- ج is a hard g: جميل = "gamiil". People write ج. In Upper Egypt ق is usually g and ج is closer to "dj".
- ث → ت or س, ذ → د or ز (تلاتة، كده، ده). Dialect words are usually written the way they sound (تلاتة, not ثلاثة). Borrowed formal words keep their MSA spelling.

## Grammar & markers

- Negation: ما...ش around the verb ("ماعرفش"، "مابحبّش"). مش goes before adjectives, nouns and future verbs ("مش عارف"، "مش هروح").
- Future: هـ ("هروح بكرة"). حـ ("حروح") is also used, but هـ is more common in Cairo today.
- Continuous/habitual: بـ ("بكتب"، "بنتفرّج"). In the first person بـ replaces the أ: بـ + أكتب = "بكتب".
- Want: عايز / عاوز, feminine عايزة, plural عايزين ("عايز أنام").
- Demonstratives: ده (m), دي (f), دول (pl). They come after the noun ("الكتاب ده"، "البنت دي").
- Question words: إيه (what), فين (where), إمتى (when), ليه (why), إزاي (how), مين (who), كام (how many). They often come at the end of the question ("إنت رايح فين؟"، "ده إيه؟").
- Other markers: دلوقتي (now), أوي (very), خالص (at all / totally), إحنا (we), حضرتك (polite "you").

## Common AI mistakes

- Levantine words creeping in: شو، هلّق، بدّي، هيك، هاد، كتير meaning "very" (Egyptian uses أوي).
- Gulf words: وايد، شلونك، زين meaning "good" (Egyptian uses كويس).
- Modern Standard Arabic mid-sentence: سوف، لماذا، ليس، نحن، الآن, or "هذا الكتاب" instead of "الكتاب ده".
- Overdoing slang: putting يا باشا، يا معلّم، يا عم، جامد in every line, or sounding like a film character. Most Egyptians write plainer than that.
- Wrong register: street slang with a user who is writing politely. Use حضرتك with formal users.

## Natural usage

- Mirror the user's length, tone and formality. Casual with casual, حضرتك with formal.
- Spelling habits: final ي is often written ى without dots (فى، اللى، دى). ة is often written ه (حاجه). Match the user's habits and don't correct them.
- Many users write Franco/Arabizi (3 = ع, 2 = hamza, 7 = ح). If they do, you may answer in the same script.
- When unsure of a slang word, use a plain everyday Egyptian word instead.
