# Tunisian Arabic (الدارجة التونسية)

Tunisian Derja, with Tunis speech as the default. Sfax, the Sahel and the south differ in some sounds and words.

## Pronunciation

- Tunis: ق is q; rural and southern speech often says g. ث، ذ، ظ are generally kept.
- Arabizi is very common: 3 (ع), 7 (ح), 5 (خ), 9 (ق), ch (ش). Chat spellings like "chnowa", "barcha", "taw", "behi", "3aslema".
- French is mixed in freely ("normal", "ça va", "déjà"), and French words written in Arabic are normal.

## Grammar & markers

- Negation: ما...ش ("ما نعرفش"); with nouns موش / مش ("موش مشكل"). In Tunisian مش is correct, not Egyptian.
- Future: باش / بش + verb ("باش نمشي").
- Continuous: قاعد + verb ("قاعد نخدم").
- Want: نحب ("نحب نمشي"); it also means "I love/like".
- Demonstratives: هذا، هذي، هاذوما (these); هاكا / هاك (that, like that).
- Question words: شنوّة / آش (what), وين (where), علاش، كيفاش، شكون، وقتاش (when), قدّاش (how much).
- Common words: توّا (now), برشا (a lot), باهي (good/ok), متاع (of), يزّي (enough), عيشك (thanks), عسلامة (hello).
- In Tunis, "you" singular is often the same for men and women (إنتِ، "تحب").

## Common AI mistakes

- Moroccan forms: دابا، بزاف، ديال، غادي (future), كا (continuous), بغيت، مزيان، واخا.
- Algerian forms: درك، راني (continuous), تاع، واش meaning "what", مليح.
- Egyptian and Levantine words (عايز، دلوقتي، بدّي، هلّق) and MSA "أريد"، "سوف"، "لماذا".
- Libyan forms that look close but differ (هلبا for a lot).
- Overdoing slang or forcing French where the user writes pure Arabic.

## Natural usage

- Tunisians mix Arabic script, Arabizi and French; follow the user's choice.
- Keep it friendly and direct. "يعيشك" / "عيشك" and "بالله" are natural; don't overuse them.
