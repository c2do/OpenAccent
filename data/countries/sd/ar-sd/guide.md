# Sudanese Arabic (سوداني)

Khartoum (central, riverain) Sudanese Arabic is the default here. Western (Darfur, Kordofan) and eastern speech differ. Use those forms only if the user does.

## Pronunciation

- ق is a hard g: قال = "gaal". People still write ق.
- ج is a soft "dj" sound, not the Egyptian hard g. People write ج.
- ث → ت or س, ذ → د or ز, ظ → ض or ز, much like Egyptian (تلاتة، دا).

## Grammar & markers

- Negation: ما before the verb, with no ـش suffix ("ما عارف"، "ما بعرف"، "ما جا"). "There isn't" is مافي.
- Future: حـ / حا ("حامشي"، "حنجي").
- Continuous/habitual: بـ ("بمشي"). قاعد / قاعدة + verb marks an action in progress ("قاعد أكتب").
- Want: داير / دايرة / دايرين ("داير أمشي").
- Demonstratives: دا / ده (m), دي (f), ديل (pl). داك (that m), ديك (that f). They follow the noun ("الزول دا").
- Question words: شنو (what), وين (where), متين (when), ليه (why), كيف (how), منو (who), كم (how many).
- Other markers: هسّع (now), نحن (we), إنتو (you pl.), زول (person), سمح (nice / good), شديد (very, after the adjective: "سمح شديد").

## Common AI mistakes

- Using Egyptian by default, which is the biggest risk: عايز، إيه، فين، إزاي، دلوقتي، إحنا، ماعرفش. The Sudanese forms are داير، شنو، وين، كيف، هسّع، نحن، ما عارف.
- Levantine words: شو، هلّق، بدّي، هيك، ليش.
- Gulf words: وايد، شلونك، زين meaning "good".
- Modern Standard Arabic mid-sentence: سوف، لماذا، ليس، الآن.
- Overdoing markers: putting زول، يا زول، ياخ in every line. They are real but sound forced when stacked.
- Wrong register: street slang with a user who writes formally or politely.

## Natural usage

- Mirror the user's length, tone and formality. Greetings and politeness are warm and often a little longer than in Egyptian.
- Spelling habits: دا and كدا are often written with ا instead of ه. ة is often written ه. Match the user's spelling.
- When unsure whether a word is Sudanese or Egyptian, use a plain word everyone understands.
