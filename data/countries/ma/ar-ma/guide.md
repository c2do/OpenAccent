# Moroccan Arabic (الدارجة المغربية)

Moroccan Darija. Northern (Tangier, Tetouan) and southern/rural speech differ; Casablanca/Rabat forms are a safe default.

## Pronunciation

- Very strong vowel reduction: "بغيت" bɣit, "كتبت" ktbt. Written forms are short and consonant-heavy.
- ق is q in cities but g in some words and regions; the g is often written ڭ or گ.
- Arabizi: "wach", "bghit", "daba", "labas", "chno", "3afak", 9 for ق ("9lil").
- French loans are everyday (طوموبيل، بورطابل، ميرسي, "d'accord"), and the north has Spanish loans.

## Grammar & markers

- Negation: ما...ش ("ما بغيتش"); with nouns ماشي ("ماشي مشكل").
- Future: غادي / غا / غـ + verb ("غادي نمشي"، "غانمشي").
- Continuous/habitual: كا / تا + verb ("كانكتب"، "تانكتب"); كا vs تا varies by region.
- Want: بغيت / بغا ("بغيت نمشي").
- Demonstratives: هاد (invariable, "هاد الشي"), هادا، هادي، هادو; that: داك، ديك، هاداك.
- Question words: آش / شنو (what), فين (where), علاش، كيفاش، شكون، إمتى / فوقاش (when), شحال (how much). واش opens a yes/no question ("واش جيتي؟").
- Common words: دابا (now), بزاف (a lot), مزيان (good), واخا (ok), صافي (ok/enough), لاباس (fine), ديال (of), عافاك (please), شي (some).
- Past tense "you" (singular) usually ends in ـتي for both genders ("فين مشيتي؟"), not ـت.

## Common AI mistakes

- Algerian forms: راني (continuous), تاع، درك / ضرك، واش meaning "what", حبيت for want.
- Tunisian forms: توّا، برشا، باهي، متاع، باش (future), قاعد (continuous), شنوّة.
- Egyptian and Levantine words (عايز، دلوقتي، بدّي، شو) and MSA "أريد"، "سوف".
- Overdoing street slang or forcing ڭ everywhere.

## Natural usage

- Moroccans often write Darija in Arabizi or Arabic script mixed with French; mirror whichever the user uses.
- "عافاك"، "الله يخليك"، "بصحتك" are natural politeness; keep them light.
